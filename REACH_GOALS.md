# Reach goals: bending golf physics

## Vision

The long-term goal is a physics-based golf roguelike where the player does not
execute a timed swing. The player chooses a club, shot, and aim, then accepts a
probability distribution generated by the physics engine.

Items should begin with recognizable golf physics and then bend those rules in
surprising ways. Realism is the foundation, not a restriction. Clear gameplay,
interesting decisions, and responsive simulation take precedence when strict
realism would make an effect confusing or uninteresting.

Extreme modifiers are also useful engineering tools. If changing a physical
quantity has no visible effect, that may reveal a hard-coded assumption or a
missing coupling in the simulator.


## Promising extreme parameters

### Rotational physics

- Moment of inertia from roughly `0.1x` to `100x`.
- Spin multiplier beyond realistic equipment limits.
- Spin-decay time from almost instant to effectively permanent.
- Magnus lift multiplier up to `10x` or more.
- Spin-axis stability, bias, or inversion.

Possible items:

- **Lead Flywheel Ball:** enormous moment of inertia; resists changes in spin.
- **Infinite Gyroscope:** spin barely decays and curvature persists through flight.
- **Curve Amplifier:** dramatically increases Magnus lift.
- **Reverse Gyro:** hook and slice directions are inverted.

The current engine does not fully propagate moment of inertia. It affects impact
friction and bounce, but not flight, spin decay, or the rolling phase. Rolling
also hard-codes the solid-sphere `5/7` slope factor. A complete inertia mechanic
should generalize spin generation, bounce, slipping, rolling acceleration, and
possibly spin decay.

### Air and gravity

- Gravity from `0.1x` to `3x`.
- Air density from near-vacuum to exaggeratedly dense air.
- Drag and lift controlled independently.
- Wind that follows, opposes, or orbits the ball.
- Wind whose direction changes after a bounce.

Possible items:

- **Moon Boots:** reduced effective gravity and long hang time.
- **Dimpled Relic:** greatly reduced drag.
- **Winged Ball:** exaggerated aerodynamic lift.
- **Tailwind Charm:** biases wind toward the current aim direction.
- **Personal Weather System:** wind follows the ball rather than the course.

### Bounce and impact

- Restitution above `1.0` for energy-adding super-bounces.
- Very high or very low impact friction.
- Exaggerated terrain deformation and crater response.
- Modified bounce-to-roll thresholds.
- Surface-dependent or impact-number-dependent behavior.

Possible items:

- **Rubber Ball:** increasingly energetic bounces.
- **Velcro Ball:** converts impact into rapid stopping.
- **Skip Stone:** preserves speed through shallow impacts and can skip on water.
- **Pinball Core:** each bounce adds energy.
- **One Good Bounce:** the first impact is perfect; later impacts are normal.

### Rolling and terrain

- Rolling resistance from near zero to extremely high.
- Negative rolling resistance for self-acceleration.
- Generalized moment-of-inertia effects during rolling.
- Slope influence below or above physical values.
- Surface substitution or blending.

Possible items:

- **Sand Skimmer:** bunker rolling behaves like fairway rolling.
- **All-Terrain Ball:** rough, bunker, and fairway share one response.
- **Ice Soles:** every surface has almost no rolling resistance.
- **Green Magnet:** resistance rises dramatically after reaching the green.
- **Uphill Both Ways:** slope forces are inverted.

### Ball properties

- Ball radius up to beach-ball scale or down to marble scale.
- Mass varied independently from radius.
- Non-spherical or direction-dependent inertia.
- Size changes during flight or after each bounce.

Possible items:

- **Beach Ball:** huge area, low density, and extreme wind response.
- **Cannonball:** high mass with weak aerodynamic response.
- **Growing Problem:** radius increases continuously during flight.
- **Russian Nesting Ball:** sheds a layer and changes properties after impact.

### Rule-breaking effects

These need not pretend to be ordinary physics:

- Water behaves like fairway for one impact.
- Out-of-bounds edges become temporary walls.
- The first hazardous result is rerolled.
- Simulate two outcomes and keep the better one.
- Mirror or duplicate the landing distribution.
- Redirect a bunker landing toward the green.
- Teleport a rough finish to the nearest fairway point.
- Apply different physics before and after the apex.
- Change parameters after every bounce.

Possible items:

- **Quantum Mulligan:** draw two physical outcomes and choose one.
- **Boundary Ward:** the first OOB crossing reflects back into play.
- **Hazard Insurance:** reroll the first water or bunker finish.
- **Mirror Ball:** creates symmetric left and right outcome branches.
- **Second-Half Ball:** changes its complete physics profile at the apex.

## Accuracy modifiers worth separating

Global dispersion is useful but mostly a strictly beneficial stat. More specific
accuracy modifiers can create distinctive items and tradeoffs:

- Ball-speed dispersion.
- Launch-angle dispersion.
- Spin-rate dispersion.
- Start-direction dispersion.
- Spin-axis dispersion.

Examples:

- **Laser Sight:** narrows start-direction dispersion.
- **Balanced Ball:** narrows spin-axis dispersion.
- **Perfect Contact:** narrows speed and launch-angle dispersion.
- **Wild Power:** increases speed while widening several dispersions.

## Architecture needed for these goals

Modifiers should eventually be grouped by phase rather than accumulated in one
flat class:

```text
PhysicsModifiers
|-- launch
|-- aerodynamics
|-- environment
|-- impact
|-- rolling
|-- surfaces
`-- rule_effects
```

Each modifier should state whether it changes:

1. The mean of a launch distribution.
2. The variance of a launch distribution.
3. Deterministic physics after launch.
4. Course or surface behavior.
5. Rules outside the physical simulation.

Club baselines, shot-type tradeoffs, item effects, and numerical-fidelity settings
should remain separate. This makes results interpretable and prevents a debugging
control from accidentally becoming part of game balance.

## Readiness milestones

Before relying on extreme item effects, generalize or validate:

- Moment of inertia through bounce, slipping, and rolling.
- Configurable drag, lift, gravity, and spin decay.
- Surface responses without mutating global constant arrays.
- Wind modifiers relative to both world direction and aim direction.
- Stable behavior at extreme parameter values.
- Conservation or intentional energy addition during impact.
- Clear handling of water and OOB rule effects.
- Item composition order when several effects modify the same quantity.

## Suggested progression

1. Add realistic launch-angle, spin, and split-dispersion modifiers.
2. Generalize aerodynamic coefficients and spin decay.
3. Move surface constants into configurable simulation state.
4. Generalize inertia-dependent rolling and slipping.
5. Add deliberately extreme laboratory ranges.
6. Promote the most legible and entertaining experiments into actual items.
7. Add explicit rule-breaking effects after the deterministic physics pipeline is
   stable enough to distinguish a feature from a bug.

The goal is not to simulate every physical detail. It is to build enough coherent
physics that breaking a rule produces an understandable, surprising, and useful
gameplay consequence.


# Synergy design

The central roguelike fantasy is not merely finding stronger equipment. It is
discovering that several strange, incomplete, or apparently harmful items combine
into a coherent and overpowered machine.

Items should interact through shared physical quantities and simulation events
rather than relying primarily on hard-coded pair bonuses. Useful shared quantities
include speed, spin, launch angle, radius, height, wind response, bounce count,
surface type, and dispersion. Useful events include launch, apex, first contact,
bounce, beginning to roll, entering a surface, leaving bounds, and stopping.

A productive synergy grammar is:

```text
Generator -> creates an unusual condition
Amplifier -> makes that condition extreme
Converter -> transforms its drawback into another advantage
Payoff    -> rewards the resulting trajectory or outcome
Safety    -> prevents the build from failing catastrophically
```

Not every build needs all five roles. The purpose of this structure is to make
items combine through the simulation. For example:

```text
More sidespin
-> slower spin decay
-> stronger Magnus curvature
-> remaining spin powers the first bounce
-> a boundary ward prevents the extreme curve from becoming OOB
```

No item in that chain needs to name another item. They form a spin build because
they generate, amplify, convert, reward, and protect the same physical state.

Promising build families include:

- **Spin:** turn hooks and slices into routing tools, preserve spin through flight,
  and convert remaining spin into landing or bounce effects.
- **Bounce:** combine low trajectories, high restitution, bounce counters, water
  skipping, and guided rebounds into a pinball-like ground game.
- **Roll:** minimize resistance and exploit slopes, then find a surface-specific
  way to stop before the ball rolls through the green.
- **Wind:** increase hang time and wind response while acquiring ways to align,
  redirect, or harvest the wind.
- **Low gravity:** reach extreme apexes, earn height-based rewards, and change
  physics during reentry.
- **Giant ball or cannonball:** combine extreme size or mass with items that cancel
  aerodynamic drawbacks and create new impact or hazard interactions.
- **Hazard:** invert course evaluation so water, bunkers, or rough become shortcuts,
  charging stations, or sources of future bonuses.
- **Boundary:** deliberately curve out of bounds, then reflect, mirror, or teleport
  back into play with additional momentum.
- **Variance:** accept or increase dispersion, then use multiple outcomes, selective
  rerolls, and rewards for extreme results to create controlled chaos.
- **Precision:** narrow specific sources of uncertainty and reward consecutive or
  extremely accurate outcomes rather than only reducing misses.
- **Surface combo:** reward touching surfaces in a particular sequence, turning one
  shot into a route-planning problem.
- **Phase change:** apply different rules before the apex, after the first impact,
  or during rolling so a shot behaves like a programmed sequence.

Good synergies should include tradeoffs. Extra spin can cause ballooning, extra
speed can overshoot, low resistance makes stopping difficult, and high variance
can produce spectacular failures. Other items should solve, redirect, or profit
from those drawbacks. A build becomes memorable when the player realizes that a
liability is actually fuel for another mechanic.

The item pool should therefore contain more than direct upgrades. It needs
enablers, specialized amplifiers, converters, conditional payoffs, and safety
tools distributed across several physical systems. This supports emergent builds
and makes the same item desirable for different reasons in different runs.
